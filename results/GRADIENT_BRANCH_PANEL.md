# Within-cell gradient regime panel — human cell 1125

## Question

The first operating-point sweep showed that geometry-gradient signs on
`apic[100]` change as the three-site NMDA circuit moves through different
drive regimes.

Is that a curiosity of one branch, or does state-dependent sign reversal recur
elsewhere in the same real neuron?

## Locked panel

- human L2/3 morphology `1125`
- full graph: **1653 compartments**
- six deterministic long apical branches
- four geometry probes per branch: x = 0.10 / 0.25 / 0.50 / 0.75
- two metric directions per probe: length / diameter
- seven drive scales: 0.25 / 0.50 / 0.75 / 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 / 3.0
- nonlinear inputs remain at x = 0.25 / 0.50 / 0.75
- **42 operating points**
- **48 geometry trajectories** followed across drive

Classification:

`WITHIN_CELL_GRADIENT_REGIME_PANEL_VALID`

## Main result

~~~text
geometry trajectories changing sign somewhere across drive

all         32 / 48
length      10 / 24
diameter    22 / 24
~~~

State-dependent geometry sensitivity is therefore not peculiar to the original
branch. It recurs across the same human dendritic tree, especially for
diameter.

But the detailed map is branch-specific.

At the released 1x drive, the proximal x≈0.1 length direction is:

~~~text
positive     4 / 6 branches
negative     2 / 6 branches
~~~

So even the visually striking pre-site warming pocket from `apic[100]` is not
a cell-wide rule.

## Diameter is especially regime-sensitive

Across the 24 diameter probes:

~~~text
drive 0.25x:    17 positive / 7 negative
drive 3.00x:    22 positive / 2 negative

trajectories that flip at least once:
                22 / 24
~~~

The direction of a local diameter perturbation is therefore generally not
predictable from morphology alone. The nonlinear operating state matters.

## A useful clue, not yet a law

At 1x drive:

- `apic[100]`: max local depolarization ≈46.65 mV; all four tested diameter
  gradients are negative.
- `apic[96]`: max local depolarization ≈64.38 mV; all four tested diameter
  gradients are positive.
- the other branches occupy mixed intermediate cases.

Across all branch/drive records, a crude threshold on **maximum local
depolarization** alone classifies individual diameter-gradient sign at about
77% accuracy (best threshold in this panel ≈58.2 mV depolarization).

That is only a diagnostic hint. It is not a validated predictor, because one
maximum voltage is being used to summarize a three-site coupled nonlinear
state.

It motivates a cleaner next test:

> split the exact geometry tangent into the contribution from the **compiled
> transport operator with the synaptic current waveform frozen** and the extra
> contribution generated by **voltage-dependent NMDA feedback**.

If sign reversals live mainly in the second term, the architecture has earned a
concrete decomposition: geometry determines transport, state-dependent local
nonlinearity determines how that geometry is valued at the current operating
point.

## Fence

This is a within-cell result. It does not establish cross-cell universality,
a biological growth rule, or a simple voltage threshold for geometry
adaptation.
