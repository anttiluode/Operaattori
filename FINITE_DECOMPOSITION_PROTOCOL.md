# Finite transport/NMDA decomposition prediction — frozen protocol

Status: **frozen before the first run of this experiment**.

Date: 2026-09-10

## Why this experiment exists

The September 2026 prior-art audit killed the broad novelty claims around differentiable morphology, Green-function reduction, morphology sensitivity, and qualitative morphology×NMDA interaction.

One narrow object remained un-killed in that pass: Operaattori's explicit first-order split

```text
full geometry derivative
    =
frozen-current transport derivative
    +
voltage-dependent NMDA-feedback derivative
```

measured on the released 24-cell FCI morphology panel.

An explanatory split is weaker than a predictive one. This experiment therefore asks:

> Can the derivative split, computed at the unperturbed state, predict which **finite** local morphology edits will have the opposite sign under self-consistent NMDA feedback than they would under frozen-current passive transport?

This is an internal reduced-model intervention test. It is **not** an external Jaxley/NEURON validation and does not establish literature priority.

## Locked source and panel

- FCI source commit: `75ad8b4d81a7f51bf888b30650c543592340db06`
- all **24 released FCI morphologies**
- one deterministic longest apical branch per morphology, exactly as in `audits/cross_cell_gradient_decomposition.py`
- nonlinear sites: `x = 0.25, 0.50, 0.75`
- local metric directions: `{length, diameter}` at each site
- **6 directions per cell per operating point**
- drive scales: `0.5, 1.0, 2.0`
- positive finite metric edits only

The source-model, branch-selection, conductance program, cable compiler, and NMDA law are inherited unchanged from the existing locked cross-cell decomposition.

## Finite edit sizes

Primary edit:

```text
q = 1.05
log(q) = log(1.05)
```

Curvature attacker:

```text
q = 1.10
log(q) = log(1.10)
```

A positive length edit changes only the selected compartment according to the already-audited metric convention:

```text
membrane area / leak / capacitance ~ q
half-axial conductance             ~ 1/q
```

A positive diameter edit uses:

```text
membrane area / leak / capacitance ~ q
half-axial conductance             ~ q^2
```

Topology and synapse addresses remain fixed.

## Three predictions for every edit

For every cell, drive and local metric direction, first compute the **unperturbed** analytic decomposition at the base soma-peak time:

```text
g_full
 g_transport
 g_feedback = g_full - g_transport
```

Then make the finite metric edit and measure two counterfactuals.

### 1. Self-consistent finite edit

Recompile `G,C` after the finite metric change and rerun the local implicit NMDA dynamics. This gives

```text
Delta_full_finite
```

at the **original base peak time**. Using a fixed time avoids changing the observable after seeing a peak shift.

### 2. Frozen-current finite transport edit

Take the nonlinear synaptic current waveform generated by the **unperturbed** base trajectory, freeze it, recompile the changed `G,C`, and replay that same current waveform through the changed passive recurrence. This gives

```text
Delta_transport_finite
```

at the same base peak time.

This is the finite counterpart of Operaattori's frozen-current transport tangent. It is a mathematical counterfactual, not a separate biological model.

### 3. First-order predictions

```text
pred_full      = g_full      * log(q)
pred_transport = g_transport * log(q)
```

## Sign definitions

Finite effects with absolute magnitude below

```text
1e-5 mV
```

are classified `near_zero` and excluded from sign precision/recall denominators. Raw values are still retained.

A tangent predicts a transport/NMDA sign reversal when `g_full` and `g_transport` are both nonzero and have opposite signs.

A finite intervention realizes that reversal when `Delta_full_finite` and `Delta_transport_finite` both exceed the finite sign threshold and have opposite signs.

## Primary preregistered endpoints at +5%

The experiment earns `FINITE_DECOMPOSITION_PREDICTIVE` only if all of the following hold across the complete 24-cell × 3-drive × 6-direction primary panel:

1. **Internal replay identity:** replaying the base current through the unperturbed compiler reproduces the base soma trace with relative RMS error `<= 1e-10` for every operating point.
2. **Full tangent finite-sign accuracy:** among non-near-zero self-consistent finite effects, `sign(g_full)` predicts `sign(Delta_full_finite)` in at least **90%** of edits.
3. **Transport tangent finite-sign accuracy:** among non-near-zero frozen-current finite effects, `sign(g_transport)` predicts `sign(Delta_transport_finite)` in at least **90%** of edits.
4. **Predicted reversal precision:** at least **75%** of tangent-predicted sign reversals that are finite-sign evaluable become actual finite sign reversals.
5. **Predicted reversal recall:** at least **75%** of actual finite sign reversals were predicted by the tangent split.

The 90% thresholds ask whether the analytic tangent remains useful at a finite 5% edit. The 75% reversal thresholds are deliberately lower because sign-reversal cases lie near a competition between two contributions and are expected to be more curvature-sensitive.

## Secondary quantitative endpoints

Always report, without changing the primary decision:

- median and 90th percentile absolute error of `pred_full` versus `Delta_full_finite`;
- median and 90th percentile relative error where the actual effect is above `1e-5 mV`;
- the same errors for transport;
- results separately for length and diameter;
- results separately at 0.5x, 1x and 2x drive;
- confusion counts for predicted versus realized reversals;
- the +10% curvature attacker using the same frozen definitions.

The +10% panel has **no separate pass threshold**. It tests how quickly the first-order decomposition loses predictive value as the edit grows.

## Kill interpretations

If ordinary full/transport finite-sign accuracy fails, the analytic tangent is too local to support the proposed finite-intervention use at 5%.

If ordinary sign accuracy passes but reversal precision/recall fails, the decomposition remains a local explanatory diagnostic but does not reliably predict the finite competition between passive transport and NMDA feedback.

If the +5% panel passes but +10% degrades sharply, the method has a finite validity radius rather than a scale-free morphology rule.

If the primary panel passes, the earned claim is only:

> On this released 24-morphology reduced-model panel, the base-state analytic transport/NMDA derivative decomposition predicts the sign of finite 5% local metric interventions, including most finite feedback-induced reversals.

It does **not** establish that the decomposition is new, biological growth follows these gradients, or the same predictions hold in an independent simulator. The next required attack would be external reproduction in Jaxley or direct NEURON finite perturbations.
